# Business Case — School IT Support Knowledge Hub

## 1. Problem / Opportunity

Schools experience frequent technology problems and usually these are solved by the School IT staff. For many problems though, School IT staff are not equipped to solve them on the spot as they would need to research and usually staff do not have time for this and thus contact IT for the same problem over and over again.

## 2. Proposed Solution

This School IT Support Knowledge Hub application is developed as a simple web application, including a list of common School IT related issues and detailed troubleshooting information for each of these issues. Staff can use the browsing function and view favorite articles by bookmarking them. These common issues will be dealt with by staff, and then referred to IT staff for more complex problems.

## 3. Options Considered

| Option | Description | Pros | Cons |
|--------|-------------|------|------|
| Option A | Continue handling technology questions through email, phone calls, and in-person support. | No development cost and staff can communicate directly with IT. | Repetitive requests take IT staff time, solutions are not always documented, and users may have to wait for assistance. |
| Option B (recommended) | Develop a web-based IT support knowledge hub containing common issues and detailed solutions. | Gives staff immediate access to common solutions, reduces repetitive support requests, organizes support information in one location, and can be expanded over time. | Requires initial development time and occasional updates when technology or procedures change. |

## 4. Feasibility

| Type | Assessment |
|------|------------|
| Operational — will people actually use/support this? | High. As staff are already searching for technology assistance the provision of a simple searchable or browsable support topics in a webpage will be a good addition. The IT staff can then direct users to the relevant article for common problems.
| Operational — will people actually use/support this? | High. Staff already need technology assistance, and a simple searchable or browsable collection of support topics would provide another convenient way to find help. IT staff could also direct users to specific articles when common issues occur. |
| Economic — does the payoff justify the cost? | High. The cost for the prototype is low, but IT time for repeat support questions could go down significantly during the school year.
| Schedule — can it be done in a useful timeframe? | High. A functional prototype with a few support topics can be designed and built within the time frame of the course and thereafter added to as necessary.

## 5. Costs & Benefits

**Costs** (one-time + ongoing):

| Item | One-time | Ongoing/year |
|------|----------|----------------|
| Initial design and development time | $600 | $0 |
| Testing and creation of support articles | $200 | $0 |
| Content updates and maintenance | $0 | $300 |
| Hosting using existing web resources | $0 | $0 |
| Total | $800 | $300 |

**Benefits** (tangible + intangible):

| Benefit | Tangible ($/time saved)? | Notes |
|---------|-----------------------------|-------|
| Benefit  | Tangible ($/time saved)?  | Notes  |
|---------|-----------------------------|-------|
| Reduced time spent responding to repetitive support requests  | $1,200/year estimated  | The value here assumes the system will save about 2 hours of IT support time per month. This time has an estimated value of $50 per hour, thus $1,200 per year. |
| Faster solutions for staff | Time saved | Users can immediately access instructions instead of waiting for IT assistance.
| Centralized support documentation | Intangible | Common troubleshooting procedures are kept in one consistent location. |
| More consistent solutions | Intangible | Our end-users receive the same high level of quality information every time they require a solution to a problem that occurs on a regular basis. |
| Improved IT availability | Intangible | IT staff can spend more time on problems that require direct technical assistance. |

**Payback period:** Approximately 11 months.

Initial cost = $800

Annual benefit = $1,200

Annual ongoing cost = $300

Using the same set of numbers, the annual net benefit equals $900 x 0.89 or 8.9% = $79.11 (rounded to two decimal places) per year.

This can be expressed as the payback period of the proposed investment, which is calculated as follows: Payback period = Initial cost / Net gain per year = $800 / $900 = 0.89 years or approximately 10.7 months.

**ROI:**

First-year benefit = $1,200

First-year total cost = $800 + $300 = $1,100.

ROI = ($1,200 - $1,100) / $1,100

ROI = $100 / $1,100

ROI = 0.091, or approximately 9.1%

## 6. Priority & Urgency

Technology support needs of schools do not disappear over the summer. In fact, the majority of the support issues that a single user experiences are typically identical to those that other users with similar equipment and applications experience. Thus, by waiting until the end of the school year to begin work on this project, IT staff would continue to spend their time addressing individual support issues that could be documented once and used by others instead of designing a single application to address their needs. Also, it would be wise to identify the key issues to develop into a full application as early as possible in the design phase.

## 7. Recommendation

Proceed with Option B and make a prototype of the School IT Support Knowledge Hub.
